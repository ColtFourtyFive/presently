# Reconciliation after restoration

Schema 26 adds an internal, resumable catalog reconciliation operation. A restored publication becomes available only after a fresh current-generation semantic verification and a complete comparison of its retained catalog with its authenticated v2 monthly archive. The publication's original proof generation and recorded provenance remain unchanged.

The unit of activation is one publication. Success for one month does not activate another month. The supported profile remains a monthly v2 base with no addendum references, validator version 1, locator version 1, and existing private staging admission limits. Unsupported evidence remains unavailable.

A reconciliation job pins the full immutable publication descriptor and a fresh completed semantic run. Record pages authenticate the pinned manifest and one bounded encrypted part, compare each exact locator and original record serialization, and check every event/correction claim against the permanent request owner, center, literal fingerprint, encoding and canonicalization declaration. Captured audit aliases do not create new owners. No current attendance, visit, student, guardian or review row supplies historical evidence.

Separate ordered catalog scans reach explicit EOF and compare exact part, record and claim totals. Combined with the forward comparison and immutable unique mappings, these detect both missing and extra catalog entries. Each advance handles at most eight rows. Native query plans use the part/ordinal and publication/request indexes. Terminal count checks cap observation just beyond the supported maximum so unexpected surplus entries fail without an unbounded scan. These bounds do not establish deployed CPU or quota safety.

Locator version 1 uses the same shared serializer and deterministic partition in publication and reconciliation: at most eight records and 256 KiB of serialized record bytes per page, without crossing part boundaries. The existing zero-seeded SHA-256 chain and object property order are unchanged. Independent fixed vectors cover record, byte and part boundaries, UTF-8 and property ordering.

Each claim/checkpoint uses a native database-clock lease and exact revision, fresh proof, publication identity, history generation and maintenance barrier. The terminal transaction completes the job, inserts an immutable receipt and updates or recreates availability. A final native assertion rolls back all terminal writes unless the exact receipt is ready in the expected generation. Lost responses are resolved from retained job state, without issuing a second receipt.

The API reports `state` as retained job progress and `ready` as current-generation availability. After another restore, an old job can remain `complete` while `ready` is false. Availability checks and archived readers enforce the current history generation; they do not infer readiness from an old completion. Readers also recheck the reconciliation receipt identity after asynchronous object reads.

Restoration invalidates unfinished reconciliation work and marks availability unavailable while retaining original publications, locators and completed receipts. Fresh verification may first require bounded cleanup of the invalid private staging session because admission permits only one resident session. Completed reconciliation receipts remain valid historical evidence after staging cleanup, but do not authorize a new history generation.

The encrypted backup inventory now includes all 50 tables. The lock/count/schema/archive-reference snapshot remains four native statements. Local acceptance covers an independent encrypted restore with operational attendance source details absent, verification from downloaded archive objects, full reconciliation, exact original event/correction receipts and a second access reset. Missing or corrupt evidence, wrong ownership, changed receipt identity, incomplete catalog, interrupted checkpoints and failed terminal activation remain unavailable.

This is internal recovery infrastructure. Public archive routes, unattended reconciliation, account-loss cloud restoration and deployed capacity remain unverified or disabled. Invalid unpublished builds still retain request claims; bounded abandonment cleanup remains necessary. Historical interval/report/correction authority, addenda, holds and safe source eviction remain separate work. Live source data has not been deleted.
